# Teams hold skills; groups inside a team decide who sees them

Status: accepted (design only; nothing here is built yet. Replaces the reserved "Workspace" term and roadmap item 8)

A user belongs to any number of **teams**. A team is the boundary a skill lives in: its own vault, its own members, its own invite code. Whoever creates a team is its admin. Inside a team, **groups** such as `backend` or `security` hold skills and members, and each group is **open** (the whole team can see and use its skills) or **private** (only its members can).

The shape is GitHub's, because it is the one most people who will use this already know: an organization with owners and members, and teams inside it that are visible or secret. Organization maps to team, team to group, visible and secret to open and private, owner to team admin.

## The rules

- **An account is created once per server**, with the server's invite code, exactly as `join` works today (ADR-0003). That creates the user's personal team: an ordinary team with one member. Nothing in the code treats it specially, and inviting a friend makes it a two-person team.
- **A team has its own invite code**, generated by the server, stored as a hash, and replaceable by a team admin. The server's code admits someone to the server; a team's code admits them to that team and nothing else.
- **A token is a person on one computer and is never shared.** A second computer gets its own token from `add_machine` and sees every team its user is in, because membership belongs to the user. A friend joins with their own account and the team's invite code. Handing someone a token would record their pushes as yours.
- **Two roles: team admin and member.** An admin manages the invite code, members, and groups, and can move a skill between groups. "Backend developer" is not a role with permissions attached; it is membership of the `backend` group.
- **A user can see a skill when** they are in its team, and the skill is at team level, or in an open group, or in a group they belong to. A team with no groups, or only open ones, is a team where everyone sees everything.
- **Access is also settable per person.** A team admin can give one member sight of every group in the team, private ones included, without making them an admin or adding them group by group. It grants reading and using, not pushing. Everyone else is limited to the groups they are in. That is the whole per-person control: see everything, or see your groups. A member may push to team level and to groups they belong to. An open group is readable by the team and writable by its members.
- **Skills are pushed flat and placed on the server.** A push that names no team goes to the pusher's personal team: that is where every skill starts, and sharing is always a deliberate second step. A push to a team may name a group; otherwise the skill lands at team level and an admin can move it. Placement is a column, not a folder: no machine's disk changes when a skill moves.
- **Provenance is unchanged and always shown.** Every revision carries `pushed_by`, the username bound to the token at join, in open groups as much as private ones. A team's skills are not an anonymous library.
- **A machine holds one skill per name.** The global skills directory is flat, so enabling `code-review` from a second team is refused with the name of the team the first came from.
- **Removing access stops listing, installing, and updating. It does not delete installed copies**, which are files on someone's disk (ADR-0001). Their next sync reports the skill as no longer available to them.

## Where the check lives

Identity already arrives on every request: the token verifier resolves the bearer to a machine and user before any tool runs, and the endpoint is stateless (ADR-0013), so there is no session to look up and nothing to read from a log. Visibility is one function in `services/` that takes the user and returns the skills they may see. Every read and every write goes through it, the same way every tool goes through one `AuthMiddleware`: a new tool is covered by default, and forgetting falls closed. A change to a membership takes effect on that user's next request.

## Not doing

- **A permission matrix** of roles × folders × read/write. It is the hardest part to get right and to explain, every bug in it is a leak, and open or private per group covers the cases that came up.
- **Nested groups.** They need inheritance and precedence rules, and nothing asked for them.
- **One machine on two servers.** Each server would see the other's skills as orphans and offer to push them. A person whose teams live on different servers is a separate piece of work.
- **Hiding who pushed.** See above.

## Consequences

- The first real schema migration. Existing skills move into a default team that every existing user is a member of, so an upgraded server behaves as it did.
- Skill names become unique per team, not per server.
- The tool count grows: create a team, get its invite code, and join a team in the first step; group management in the second. `push_skill` and `list_skills` gain a team.
- The server's first user keeps `is_admin` (ADR-0008) as the server's operator role. It grants nothing inside a team they are not in.

Rejected: sub-teams as separate, flat teams (cannot express "open within the company"); a single shared vault with tags (a tag is not a boundary, and a boundary is what was asked for).
